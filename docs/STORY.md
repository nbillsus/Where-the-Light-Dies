# Where the Light Dies - Story Bible

## Narrative Intent

Where the Light Dies should feel like a ghost story told through weather, radio static, and the slow return of a signal that may not be entirely human. The player should rarely be stopped for exposition. Instead, story arrives through short transmissions, environmental details, sudden noises, false rescue calls, and visual changes as the island's beacon system comes back online.

The Keeper is not a superhero. They are the last person awake in a failing lighthouse, trying to make sense of a blackout that has swallowed the coast. Their detached Shadow is frightening at first, but it becomes an uneasy partner: a survival instinct given shape. The horror should come from uncertainty: sometimes the Shadow protects the Keeper, sometimes it seems to hear something the Keeper cannot.

## Core Premise

A storm cuts the lighthouse island off from the mainland. The main lens is shattered, the relay stations are dead, and a dark fog is spreading across the coast. When the shortwave radio catches a broken distress call, The Keeper discovers that the lighthouse is the only landmark a rescue vessel can use to pass the reefs safely.

The lighthouse was originally built after a wreck known as the Mourning Reef disaster, when dozens of voices were heard calling from the fog for three nights after the ship had already sunk. The first keepers found refracting sea crystals under the cliff and built the beacon to split its light through them, creating a wide warning field that kept ships away from the reef and held back whatever lived in the fog.

Years later, that origin has become a half-forgotten local story. The modern Keeper knows the lighthouse as a lonely job, not a seal over something ancient. When the main lens goes dark, the old voices return. They imitate the living. They cry for help in familiar tones. They use the radio. They use the Keeper's own voice.

## Key Characters

### The Keeper

The player character. Wears a weathered ochre oilskin coat, carries a brass storm lantern, and knows the lighthouse well enough to move through it in darkness. The Keeper is practical, tired, and stubborn. Their fear shows in hesitation and breathing, not long speeches.

### The Detached Shadow

The Keeper's shadow after it separates during the opening lightning strike. It moves like smoke under tension: curious, wary, and sometimes almost protective. It does not speak, but it reacts to radio voices, beacon light, and deep darkness.

### The Radio Voice

A distant coastal operator and later a rescue ship contact. Early transmissions are distorted and incomplete. As relay stations return, the voice becomes clearer, revealing the stakes and guiding the player toward the main lens. Some transmissions are real. Some are copied by the fog. The player should slowly learn to listen for wrong details: reversed breathing, doubled words, a voice that asks to be let in.

### The Previous Crew

Not present in the game except through logs, abandoned tools, marked crystal diagrams, and short recorded fragments. Their work explains the island's light system without turning the story into a lecture.

### The Drowned Choir

The presence inside the fog. It is not a single monster, but a collection of voices and shadows left around the reef. It does not simply attack; it lures, repeats, knocks, begs, and waits for the player to turn around.

## Act Structure

### Prologue - The Lighthouse Interior

The opening shows the lighthouse as a dying machine: cold stairs, flickering candles, wet stone, and the dead main beacon above. The first radio burst warns that the fog is spreading, then cuts into a child's whisper saying the Keeper's name. Lightning reveals the Keeper's shadow twitching independently before it detaches.

Story beat: the player learns that the island is not only haunted by darkness - it was built to keep the darkness offshore.

### Act 1 - The Storm-Lashed Pier & Boathouse

The Keeper restores the first coastal relay near flooded docks and hanging lamps. The radio clears enough to confirm that a rescue ship is nearby but blind in the storm. Between real calls, the player hears desperate cries from below the pier: "Help me. I can see your light." The cries stop whenever the lantern points toward them.

Story beat: the Keeper is no longer only trying to survive. They are responsible for guiding others through the reefs.

### Act 2 - The Crystal Sea Caves & Sunken Chapel

Old prism mechanisms and stained glass reveal how the previous crew focused light through the island. The second relay uncovers recorded crew fragments describing how the fog reacts to concentrated light. The cave walls carry old tally marks from keepers who counted the voices every night until the count became impossible.

Story beat: the Shadow is reframed from a curse into part of the island's light system, able to redirect beams where the Keeper cannot stand. The Shadow may be the lighthouse's last defense, not an enemy.

### Act 3 - The Cliffside Ascent & Main Beacon

Two-way contact is restored. The rescue ship is close, but the reef passage is impossible without the main lighthouse beam. The Keeper must climb back through the storm, repair the lens path, and ignite the beacon before the ship is lost. The Drowned Choir uses the clearest signal yet to imitate the Keeper crying for help from rooms the player just left.

Story beat: the final return to the lighthouse turns the opening image inside out. The dead tower becomes a signal again, and the copied voices vanish under the real horn of the rescue ship.

## Opening Cutscene Beat Sheet

1. Wide ocean: black waves, rain sheets, and a single unlit lighthouse on jagged cliffs.
2. Lantern room: the Keeper sits beside a dying lamp, a cracked lens overhead, and a shortwave radio on a table.
3. Radio burst: harsh static, then a broken voice: "Anyone on the coast... the fog is spreading... if the beacon doesn't light before..."
4. Lightning flash: shadows on the walls move against the direction of the light.
5. The wrong voice: the radio whispers the Keeper's name in the Keeper's own voice.
6. Separation: the Keeper's own shadow twitches, pulls loose from their feet, and rises into a smoky silhouette.
7. First scare: something pounds once from inside the locked lens room above, then goes silent.
8. Player handoff: the lantern flares, the radio cuts out, and control begins with the Keeper facing the first dark stairwell.

## Jump-Scare Story Beats

Jump scares should be tied to the story, not random noise. Each major scare should either reveal that the fog can imitate voices, prove the lighthouse has been sealing something away, or show that the Shadow notices danger first.

### Prologue - Lens Room Knock

Setup: after the first radio whisper, the music drops until only rain and the lantern flame remain.

Scare: a violent pound comes from the dead lens room above, shaking dust loose from the ceiling.

Aftermath: when the player reaches the door, nothing is behind it except wet footprints facing inward.

### Act 1 - Cry Beneath the Pier

Setup: a muffled cry for help comes from under the planks. It sounds human and close.

Scare: when the player approaches a broken gap, a black hand slaps up through the boards with a sharp wet impact.

Aftermath: the cry repeats once from farther away, now in the Keeper's voice.

### Act 2 - Chapel Reflection

Setup: stained glass reflections show the Keeper's silhouette delayed by half a second.

Scare: the delayed reflection turns toward the camera/player before the real Keeper does, accompanied by a glass shriek.

Aftermath: the Shadow flinches away from the reflection, hinting that it knows the image is wrong.

### Act 3 - Help From Behind

Setup: while climbing back to the lighthouse, the radio plays a clean recording of the Keeper crying, "Help me, please, I am still down here."

Scare: if the player turns back or reaches a landing, the cry cuts into a sudden close whisper directly behind the player: "You came back."

Aftermath: the storm audio returns too loudly, making the player feel exposed.

## Player Cry / Help Voice Lines

These lines are for story-dependent vocal moments. They can be recorded as the Keeper, copied by the fog, or distorted through the radio depending on the scene.

- Prologue fear: "Is someone up there?"
- Prologue panic: "No, no, stay in the light."
- Act 1 lure: "Help me. I can see your lantern."
- Act 1 copied voice: "Please, I am under the pier."
- Act 2 realization: "That is not my reflection."
- Act 2 pressure: "It knows my voice."
- Act 3 false distress: "Help me, please, I am still down here."
- Act 3 final push: "Keep burning. Just keep burning."

## Radio Transmission Script

### Transmission 1 - Prologue Distress

Trigger: first shortwave interaction after the Shadow detaches.

> "...coast station to any light... repeat, any light still burning? We have vessels blind in the fog. The main beacon is dark. If anyone is on that island, answer."
>
> "[Keeper name]..."

Tone: thin, broken, mostly static. Only key nouns should cut through clearly: "coast," "light," "fog," "beacon." The name whisper should sound close, dry, and wrong, as if it is not coming from the radio speaker.

### Transmission 2 - Act 1 Relay Restored

Trigger: pier relay restored.

> "This is rescue vessel Caldera. We can see the cliffs only when lightning hits. There are reefs on both sides and something in the fog is pulling sound away. If the lighthouse can hear us, we need your beam."
>
> "Help me. I can see your lantern."

Tone: still distorted, but readable. Add low engine rumble and rain over the voice. The help cry should be mixed lower than the real transmission, almost hidden under dock creaks.

### Transmission 3 - Act 2 Relay Restored

Trigger: cave relay restored.

> "Crew log, Keeper's archive. The crystals do not create light. They remember it, bend it, carry it where flame cannot go. The dark recoils only when the beam is held steady. Do not answer any voice that comes from below the water. Do not answer your own."

Tone: archival, warmer, with tape flutter and age. This should feel like a recorded message from the previous crew.

### Transmission 4 - Act 3 Contact

Trigger: final cliffside relay restored.

> "Lighthouse, this is Caldera. We have your signal. Barely. The reef channel is ahead and we cannot hold position much longer. Bring the main lens online and keep it burning. We will follow your light home."
>
> "Help me, please, I am still down here."

Tone: urgent and clear. Static should drop out for the real final sentence, then return as the false Keeper cry. The fake cry should be intimate and unprocessed at first, then smear into radio static.

## Environmental Storytelling Checklist

- Wet boot tracks and dropped tools should imply the previous crew left in a hurry.
- Scratched warning phrases near old relay panels should mention not answering voices from the water.
- Crystal diagrams should appear first as background markings, then as readable clues near Act 2.
- The radio should visually improve as relays return: less flicker, steadier tuning needle, cleaner speaker glow.
- As the signal improves, the fake voices should also become more convincing.
- Warm amber light marks safety, memory, and rescue.
- Cold blue light marks storm exposure, isolation, and the unknown.
- Pure black silhouettes mark active threat or puzzle darkness.
