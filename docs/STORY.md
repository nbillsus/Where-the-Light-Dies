# Where the Light Dies - Story Bible

## Narrative Intent

Where the Light Dies should feel like a ghost story told through weather, radio static, and the slow return of a signal. The player should rarely be stopped for exposition. Instead, story arrives through short transmissions, environmental details, and visual changes as the island's beacon system comes back online.

The Keeper is not a superhero. They are the last person awake in a failing lighthouse, trying to make sense of a blackout that has swallowed the coast. Their detached Shadow is frightening at first, but it becomes an uneasy partner: a survival instinct given shape.

## Core Premise

A storm cuts the lighthouse island off from the mainland. The main lens is shattered, the relay stations are dead, and a dark fog is spreading across the coast. When the shortwave radio catches a broken distress call, The Keeper discovers that the lighthouse is the only landmark a rescue vessel can use to pass the reefs safely.

The island was built over a vein of refracting sea crystals once used by an earlier crew to amplify the beacon. The crystals can focus light strongly enough to repel the living darkness inside the fog, but the old system must be restored one relay at a time.

## Key Characters

### The Keeper

The player character. Wears a weathered ochre oilskin coat, carries a brass storm lantern, and knows the lighthouse well enough to move through it in darkness. The Keeper is practical, tired, and stubborn. Their fear shows in hesitation and breathing, not long speeches.

### The Detached Shadow

The Keeper's shadow after it separates during the opening lightning strike. It moves like smoke under tension: curious, wary, and sometimes almost protective. It does not speak, but it reacts to radio voices, beacon light, and deep darkness.

### The Radio Voice

A distant coastal operator and later a rescue ship contact. Early transmissions are distorted and incomplete. As relay stations return, the voice becomes clearer, revealing the stakes and guiding the player toward the main lens.

### The Previous Crew

Not present in the game except through logs, abandoned tools, marked crystal diagrams, and short recorded fragments. Their work explains the island's light system without turning the story into a lecture.

## Act Structure

### Prologue - The Lighthouse Interior

The opening shows the lighthouse as a dying machine: cold stairs, flickering candles, wet stone, and the dead main beacon above. The first radio burst warns that the fog is spreading. Lightning reveals the Keeper's shadow twitching independently before it detaches.

Story beat: the player learns that the island is not only haunted by darkness - it is needed by people outside the island.

### Act 1 - The Storm-Lashed Pier & Boathouse

The Keeper restores the first coastal relay near flooded docks and hanging lamps. The radio clears enough to confirm that a rescue ship is nearby but blind in the storm.

Story beat: the Keeper is no longer only trying to survive. They are responsible for guiding others through the reefs.

### Act 2 - The Crystal Sea Caves & Sunken Chapel

Old prism mechanisms and stained glass reveal how the previous crew focused light through the island. The second relay uncovers recorded crew fragments describing how the fog reacts to concentrated light.

Story beat: the Shadow is reframed from a curse into part of the island's light system, able to redirect beams where the Keeper cannot stand.

### Act 3 - The Cliffside Ascent & Main Beacon

Two-way contact is restored. The rescue ship is close, but the reef passage is impossible without the main lighthouse beam. The Keeper must climb back through the storm, repair the lens path, and ignite the beacon before the ship is lost.

Story beat: the final return to the lighthouse turns the opening image inside out. The dead tower becomes a signal again.

## Opening Cutscene Beat Sheet

1. Wide ocean: black waves, rain sheets, and a single unlit lighthouse on jagged cliffs.
2. Lantern room: the Keeper sits beside a dying lamp, a cracked lens overhead, and a shortwave radio on a table.
3. Radio burst: harsh static, then a broken voice: "Anyone on the coast... the fog is spreading... if the beacon doesn't light before..."
4. Lightning flash: shadows on the walls move against the direction of the light.
5. Separation: the Keeper's own shadow twitches, pulls loose from their feet, and rises into a smoky silhouette.
6. Player handoff: the lantern flares, the radio cuts out, and control begins with the Keeper facing the first dark stairwell.

## Radio Transmission Script

### Transmission 1 - Prologue Distress

Trigger: first shortwave interaction after the Shadow detaches.

> "...coast station to any light... repeat, any light still burning? We have vessels blind in the fog. The main beacon is dark. If anyone is on that island, answer."

Tone: thin, broken, mostly static. Only key nouns should cut through clearly: "coast," "light," "fog," "beacon."

### Transmission 2 - Act 1 Relay Restored

Trigger: pier relay restored.

> "This is rescue vessel Caldera. We can see the cliffs only when lightning hits. There are reefs on both sides and something in the fog is pulling sound away. If the lighthouse can hear us, we need your beam."

Tone: still distorted, but readable. Add low engine rumble and rain over the voice.

### Transmission 3 - Act 2 Relay Restored

Trigger: cave relay restored.

> "Crew log, Keeper's archive. The crystals do not create light. They remember it, bend it, carry it where flame cannot go. The dark recoils only when the beam is held steady. Do not trust open shadow unless you have anchored it yourself."

Tone: archival, warmer, with tape flutter and age. This should feel like a recorded message from the previous crew.

### Transmission 4 - Act 3 Contact

Trigger: final cliffside relay restored.

> "Lighthouse, this is Caldera. We have your signal. Barely. The reef channel is ahead and we cannot hold position much longer. Bring the main lens online and keep it burning. We will follow your light home."

Tone: urgent and clear. Static should drop out for the final sentence.

## Environmental Storytelling Checklist

- Wet boot tracks and dropped tools should imply the previous crew left in a hurry.
- Crystal diagrams should appear first as background markings, then as readable clues near Act 2.
- The radio should visually improve as relays return: less flicker, steadier tuning needle, cleaner speaker glow.
- Warm amber light marks safety, memory, and rescue.
- Cold blue light marks storm exposure, isolation, and the unknown.
- Pure black silhouettes mark active threat or puzzle darkness.
